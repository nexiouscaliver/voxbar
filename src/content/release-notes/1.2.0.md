## Spoken Commands Land Reliably

The voice command pipeline got a reliability pass. Saying "comma" when the model already wrote the comma no longer gives you two: the mark that is already there is kept and the duplicate is skipped. Punctuation attached to a command word ("question mark?") now converts exactly once. Saying a symbol twice on purpose inserts it twice, while the same symbol repeated across two separate bursts still collapses to one, as before.

Quick hotkey releases stopped eating word ends. Streaming recordings now keep a short 200 ms capture tail after you release the key, so the last word still makes it into the transcript. The tail applies to every streaming session, with or without VAD, and Settings, under Output, "Streaming Release Tail" can turn it down or off.

The command pipeline also stopped swallowing innocent words. A word that merely starts like a command ("computer" after an unfinished "com") now returns to your dictation instead of being discarded. Words the engine rewrites mid-stream are absorbed quietly instead of being misread as commands, and editing your text mid-dictation (deleting a word, retyping) no longer duplicates or drops text when the final transcript folds in.

## Commands Speak Hindi and Hinglish

The built-in command words now include Devanagari and romanized Hindi spellings, so "komaa" or its Devanagari form inserts a comma when your output language is Hindi or Hinglish, on streaming and batch models alike. Chinese output gains the core set (comma, period, question mark, exclamation, colon, semicolon, dash, new line, new paragraph). English dictation is untouched: the new aliases never match English words.

One catch: if you edited the command table in an earlier version, your stored phrases are kept as they are. Use Reset all commands in Settings, under Commands, to pick up the new built-in phrases.

## Plain Speech Is Safer

Everyday words that collide with command names no longer convert mid-sentence. "A period of time", "five star hotel", "ten percent higher" and "pipe dream" stay words; saying the command word at the very end of a sentence still converts ("I will be there period").

"Start over" no longer wipes your dictation. The clear command now listens for "delete everything" and "scratch everything", and you can add back any phrase you prefer in the Commands tab. If you edited the command table before upgrading, "start over" keeps its old meaning in your stored table until you reset it.

## A Dedicated Commands Tab

All 30 spoken commands now live in their own Settings tab, right after General, grouped into Punctuation, Symbols, Editing and Control, with a search field at the top. Each command shows the phrases it listens for and what it inserts. Phrases can be added and removed (removal offers an undo), each command can be reset on its own, and Reset all commands restores the built-in set behind a confirmation. Duplicate phrases and over-long entries are flagged on the row before anything is saved. The four command behavior toggles (auto-interpret commands, spoken punctuation, terminal punctuation, voice deletion) moved onto this tab too.

## Settings, Reorganized

Nothing was removed, things just moved closer to where you look for them. The pasting controls (paste method, auto submit, both paste delays, reliable paste, trailing space) and the recording controls (hold threshold, extra buffer, streaming tail) now live on a new Output tab. General gained the app behavior rows (start hidden, launch at login, tray icon, overlay) and a microphone group, and the sound theme picker now sits with the other sound settings. History keeps its retention settings at the top of its own tab. Advanced slimmed down to model and memory management, the transcription passes, and the single Experimental group, and Debug holds only actual diagnostics.

There is also a search field above the settings tabs: type a setting's name, a group name, or a command phrase, and jump straight to the section that owns it.

## Numbers As Digits By Default

Spoken numbers now write themselves as digits. "One eight zero one" pastes 1801, "pull request one one zero five" pastes "pull request 1105", and "twenty five" pastes 25, which means "twenty dash five" now yields 25 rather than twenty-five. Number words that are just words ("no one knows", "give me five", "nine out of ten") stay words, list speech like "one, two, three" stays a list, and existing digits or names like "iPhone 15" are never touched. Hindi and Hinglish dictation gets the same treatment: Devanagari number words become digits, romanized in Hinglish output. If you prefer the old behavior, Settings, under Commands, "Spoken Numbers" has an "As transcribed" option that keeps every number exactly as spoken; its Smart option additionally writes compound ordinals ("the twenty fifth of March" becomes "the 25th of March") and lone numbers from eleven up.
