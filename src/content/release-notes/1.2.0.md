## Spoken Commands Land Reliably

- **No more double punctuation.** Saying "comma" when the mark is already there keeps the existing one and skips the duplicate; "question mark?" converts exactly once.
- **Repeat symbols on purpose.** The same symbol said twice in a row inserts twice; the same symbol across two separate bursts still collapses to one.
- **Quick releases keep word ends.** Streaming recordings keep a 200 ms capture tail after you release the key, so the last word makes it in (Settings, Output, "Streaming Release Tail" can turn it down or off).
- **Innocent words survive.** A word that merely starts like a command ("computer" after an unfinished "com") returns to your dictation; engine mid-stream rewrites are absorbed quietly; editing text mid-dictation no longer duplicates or drops words.

## Commands Speak Hindi and Hinglish

- **Devanagari and romanized Hindi command words** ("komaa" and its Devanagari form) now insert punctuation for Hindi and Hinglish output, on streaming and batch models alike.
- **Chinese output gains the core set:** comma, period, question mark, exclamation, colon, semicolon, dash, new line, new paragraph. English dictation is untouched.
- **Edited command tables keep your phrases.** Use Reset all commands in Settings, under Commands, to pick up the new built-ins.

## Plain Speech Is Safer

- **Everyday phrases stay words.** "A period of time", "five star hotel", "ten percent higher" and "pipe dream" no longer convert; a command word at the very end of a sentence still does ("I will be there period").
- **"Start over" no longer wipes your dictation.** The clear command now listens for "delete everything" and "scratch everything"; add back any phrase you prefer in the Commands tab. Previously edited tables keep "start over"'s old meaning until you reset them.

## A Dedicated Commands Tab

- **All 30 spoken commands in one place**, right after General, grouped into Punctuation, Symbols, Editing and Control, with a search field at the top.
- **Every command shows its phrases and what it inserts.** Phrases can be added and removed (removal offers an undo), each command resets on its own, and Reset all commands restores the built-ins behind a confirmation.
- **Bad input is flagged on the row** before anything is saved: duplicate phrases and over-long entries.
- **The four behavior toggles moved here too:** auto-interpret commands, spoken punctuation, terminal punctuation, voice deletion.

## Settings, Reorganized

- **Nothing was removed; things moved closer to where you look.** Pasting and recording controls now live on a new Output tab; General gained the app behavior rows and a microphone group; History keeps its retention settings on its own tab.
- **Advanced and Debug slimmed down** to model and memory management, the transcription passes, the single Experimental group, and actual diagnostics.
- **Search above the settings tabs:** type a setting's name, a group name, or a command phrase and jump straight to the section that owns it.

## Numbers As Digits By Default

- **Spoken numbers write themselves as digits.** "One eight zero one" pastes 1801, "pull request one one zero five" pastes "pull request 1105", "twenty five" pastes 25 (so "twenty dash five" now yields 25).
- **Words that are just words stay words:** "no one knows", "give me five", "nine out of ten", list speech like "one, two, three", and existing digits or names like "iPhone 15" are never touched.
- **Hindi and Hinglish get the same treatment:** Devanagari number words become digits, romanized in Hinglish output.
- **Prefer the old behavior?** Settings, under Commands, "Spoken Numbers" has an "As transcribed" option; its Smart option additionally writes compound ordinals ("the twenty fifth of March" becomes "the 25th of March") and lone numbers from eleven up.
