## VoxBar Gets Its Own Look

VoxBar has a visual identity of its own. A new logo leads the app everywhere it appears: onboarding, the settings window, the tray, and the dock. Colors, spacing, and typography were pulled into one system, so every screen reads as the same product instead of a collection of parts written at different times. The first-run experience got the same treatment: the model picker you meet on day one carries the new identity too.

## The Recording Card Tells You What Happened

The overlay card that tracks a live dictation now carries notices of its own. When something needs your attention during or right after a dictation, the message appears on the card where your eyes already are: what ran, what succeeded, what failed, and what the app did about it. Failures no longer live only in the log file, and you no longer have to reconstruct what happened from a toast that may already have faded. The card stays quiet when there is nothing to say.

## A Polish Pass All Over

Settings, the tray menu, and the overlay each got a detail pass. Rows line up, labels agree with each other, spacing settles where it used to jump, and the tray reads cleanly at a glance. Nothing here changes what the app does. It changes how the app feels while doing it, which turns out to matter every single day.

## A README Of Its Own

The README was rewritten to present VoxBar as its own project. What it is, what it does well, where it falls short, and how to install and start using it, written for someone meeting VoxBar for the first time rather than someone who already knows its history. The troubleshooting guides were reorganized to match, so the fix you need is where the README says it is.

## Speaking 26 Languages

The interface now ships with full translation parity across 26 languages. Every user-facing string, from the first permission request to the deepest settings row, is translated, and the new screens arrive translated rather than catching up a release later.
