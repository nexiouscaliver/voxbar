## The Phone Actually Hears You Now

Companion dictation could capture silence on the phones people actually
have: iOS Safari starts any audio context created outside a user gesture
in a suspended state, so a session launched from the phone delivered no
audio at all. The phone page now creates and resumes its audio graph
inside the press itself, so the first press of a fresh page already
carries live audio. A session that ends with nothing captured is named on
both the phone and the Mac instead of silently pasting empty text, and a
microphone failure on the phone shows an inline error with a retry button
rather than recording silence.

## Where The Transcript Lands

Every phone session now chooses its destination. To PC pastes on the Mac
as before; To Phone delivers the transcript back to the phone as a card
you can copy, with a Polished badge when the polish ran, and it tries the
clipboard automatically on arrival. A per-session Polish text control
runs the post-process leg from the phone. The choices persist on the
phone and are locked in at the moment you press, so a recording already
under way keeps the shape it started with. The phone also gained its own
recent list: the last five phone dictations, with copy buttons. Hold to
talk, tap to lock, or toggle, matching your keyboard shortcut behavior.

## Pairing Beyond One Wi-Fi

Settings now shows every candidate network interface with its OS name and
a classification: LAN, Tailscale, or VPN. Automatic picks the LAN first,
and a phone on your tailnet works too, because the Tailscale address range
(100.64.0.0/10) is a first-class interface, so pairing works with no
shared Wi-Fi. You can also pin a specific interface, set the port, and
override the advertised host or IP for proxies and odd networks; when the
running server no longer matches the saved configuration, Settings says a
restart is pending and offers the button. TLS options cover the built-in
self-signed certificate, with its fingerprint shown so you can check the
browser warning the first time, or your own certificate pair.

## Notifications That Stay

Errors and session outcomes now post as real macOS notifications that
stay in Notification Center until you dismiss them, so a failed
transcription or a finished phone session can no longer flash by and be
lost. Clicking one raises the window and navigates: errors land on the
Notifications section, session outcomes on History. The master toggle is
on by default, and turning it off restores the previous behavior exactly,
including the in-app log; a separate toggle decides whether outcome
notifications join errors, and the log keeps the last fifty notices with
a Clear all button.

## Post-Processing You Can Prove

Post-processing changes are now visible instead of taken on faith. A
history entry whose text was polished shows the final pasted text and a
View changes expander with the raw-to-processed diff. Prompt Studio, in
Settings, is an evaluation bench: pick a source transcript, any number of
templates, and any number of engines, then run the whole batch. Each
result card shows the polished text, a diff against the source, the
outcome, and the latency, and can be re-run on its own. Studio runs use
the same engine pipeline as a real dictation, but nothing is typed into
other apps and nothing is saved to history. Every built-in template now
opens with a shared voice-transcription preamble that tells the engine the
input is an ASR transcript: fix homophones, spoken punctuation, and
hallucinated fragments first, never invent content. The library also
gained a Prompt Enhancer, which turns a dictated instruction into a
structured prompt for an AI agent.

## Tray And Overlay, Upgraded

The tray menu is status-first: what the app is doing right now (Idle,
Recording, Transcribing, with Cancel while busy), then live facts, then
controls. A models block lists the resident voice model with its live RAM
use and the warm local post-process model when one is held, refreshing
every ten seconds. Recent Dictations lists the last five entries, newest
first, and clicking one copies its final text. A companion block shows On
or Off, the paired device count, and where the next phone session's
transcript will land, and the Model, Unload After, Post-process Model,
and Post-process Prompt submenus switch models, the idle timer, and
templates without opening the app. On the overlay, the post-process badge
names the active template and stays honest through selection changes,
pressing the polish hotkey mid-dictation flips the live session's polish
on or off, and a cycle hotkey steps through your template library from
the keyboard, confirming the pick with its position, for example 2 of 5.
