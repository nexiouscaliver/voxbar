## Post-Processing, Reborn

Post-processing was rebuilt from a box you configured and hoped into a system you can see and steer. Local language models now download and swap exactly like voice models: pick one in Settings, under Post Process, watch it download with live progress, and switch between models as your needs change. Language-model GGUFs (Qwen, Llama, Gemma, Phi) can be added straight from a public Hugging Face repo.

Cloud providers got honest at the same time. Selecting a provider now fetches its real model list, so you choose from models that actually exist on your account instead of typing an identifier and hoping. "Test connection" exercises the whole path and reports back in plain terms: authentication, latency, and whether the selected model answers.

## Every Run, Visible End To End

Every post-processing run is now visible from start to finish. The recording card shows the run while it happens, and each history entry records the engine, the prompt, the latency, and the outcome. A failure names its reason instead of leaving you to compare pasted text with memory. The transcript is never lost along the way: if a run fails, times out, or returns unusable text, the raw transcript is kept and delivered, and the notice says exactly that.

## A Prompt Library In Your Language

Post-processing prompts now come with a built-in library covering languages and registers. English gets Professional, Casual, Technical, and Minimal templates, and the library extends past it: Hindi in Devanagari, Hinglish in roman script, Marathi, Tamil, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, and Chinese. Every template can be tested against your last transcript with the before and after side by side, so you know what a prompt does before you trust it with real dictation. Templates are versioned, and your edits are kept as the built-ins improve.

## Your Phone As A Microphone

A phone or tablet on the same Wi-Fi can now act as this Mac's microphone and trigger. Enable Companion Devices in Settings, under Advanced, scan the pairing code with the phone camera, and dictate from across the room with the phone in hand: it hears you, and it starts and stops sessions on your terms. The feature is off by default: nothing listens and no threads run until you turn it on, and pairing can be reset at any time.

## Command Mode With One Key

Command mode now accepts a lone left Command as its modifier. Hold just that key while dictating and everything you say is treated as an edit (question mark, comma, new line, delete word); release it and you are back to normal dictation, with the final paste delivering exactly the edited buffer. As before, words that are not commands are discarded while the key is held, and the mode never starts a recording on its own. Assign the key in Settings, under General, "Command Mode".

## Apple Intelligence, For Real

This is the first release with real Apple Intelligence support. Where the OS provides it, meaning an Apple Silicon Mac running macOS Tahoe (26.0) or later with Apple Intelligence enabled, Apple Intelligence works as a post-processing provider with no API key and no download, running on device. Where the OS does not provide it, VoxBar says so plainly instead of failing cryptically.
